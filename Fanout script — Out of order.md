## 2. Learnings

**Used by the reference (we take these):**

1. The hook is a paradox with a number, aimed at a belief the viewer already holds ("10× faster"). No intro, no name, no product, first word is "If".
2. A named law from a named year gives the whole piece authority in four seconds.
3. Plain-words restatement right after the law, so nobody is lost.
4. The viewer's own numbers (your week, your hours). Identification beats explanation.
5. Twist after the answer ("even infinite… probably worse") keeps the back half alive.
6. One external receipt with a count in it.
7. The closer hands the viewer a line to say to someone else. That is the share trigger. It also loops back to the hook.
8. Overlays live top-centre, captions one word at a bottom, presenter never covered. Hand-drawn red circles on the chart feel human.
9. The caption under the post asks a question, which is where the 235 comments come from.

**Not used by the reference (also learnings, for a brand piece):**

10. No CTA and no product. It is a pure awareness piece. Ours needs a soft CTA with risk reversal, placed *after* the repeatable line so the line still gets shared.
11. 102 s and two proofs (Concorde, then the week) where one would do. An ad version keeps the week and cuts the Concorde.
12. The formula slide at 18–22 s is a beat she tells you to skip ("there's a formula, but you can just think of it as"). We drop the formula entirely.
13. No social proof about her. Fanout has it (20K+, weekly, logo strip), so we use it, once, briefly.
14. The emoji equations are the generic overlay kind we don't do. Same job, our elements: Instrument Serif title lines and real screen recordings.

## 5. Debug pass — what is wrong, in the reference and in ours

**In the reference**

- The hook says *your team*, the proof is about *your week*. Ours keeps one subject: you, your level, your tabs.
- The formula slide is dead air: she shows it and tells you to ignore it. We have no formula beat.
- Two proofs for one idea. We have one (the tabs and the queue).
- Emoji stacks are the generic overlay style. Same job done here by title lines and real recordings.
- No CTA, no product. Fine for a creator, not for a brand.

**In ours (fixed or flagged)**

- *"Four hundred tabs"* is the presenter's own number. It must be sayable; otherwise use the presenter's real one. Keep it specific, it is the identification beat.
- *"eighteen thousand requests a second"* / *"the queue becomes the breakpoint"*: fanout.sh homepage Labs instrument, verified 2026-10-29.
- The 20,000 count is out (Abik, 2026-10-30). No social-proof number in speech; the logo pops are not used either, since nothing in the script claims them.
- *"Updated every week"*: verified on the homepage ("maintained weekly").
- Fast-scroll lesson titles are real (fanout.sh/inference-eng, 2026-10-29): *Build a KV Cache*, *Continuous Batching*, *Find Saturation, SLO Failure & OOM Boundaries*.
- "Not a course" rule: our copy never calls Fanout a course. fanout's own public pages do call the tracks "courses"; the house rule for ad copy stays.
- Pace: about 90 words in 29 s, 3.2 words a second.
- The word "free" is out (Abik, 2026-10-30). CTA is "Start at step one", no risk-reversal word.
- Plain words: "fell over in production" replaces the queue / requests-a-second line in speech; the Labs 18k instrument still shows on screen.
- *"build a demo in a day"* and *"fell over in production"* are the presenter's own story. Must be sayable.
- Rules check: hook in 4 s with no product ✔ · one desire (status) ✔ · one keyword per stack ✔ · product first appears at 15 s ✔ · no receipt number (removed on request) ✔ · CTA without "free" ✔ · no text on face ✔ · no new graphic elements (titles, pill, recordings, b-roll with cutout, logo pops, white card, loader) ✔ · first-person, warm ✔ · nothing copied from the reference's subject (no Amdahl, no coding speed, no week math) ✔.
