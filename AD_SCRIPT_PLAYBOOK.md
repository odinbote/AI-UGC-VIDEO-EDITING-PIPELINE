# AI UGC ad playbook — what an AI UGC / advertising video must contain

Distilled 2026-10-29 from five AI Video Bootcamp (AVB) guides Abik supplied:
*Ad Scriptwriting Frameworks 2026* (16 frameworks, 66 pp), *Ad Strategy Guide 2026*
(awareness levels × ad objectives), *Advertising Psychology Playbook* (10 principles,
after Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising*), *Hook Library* (100+ openers) and
*UGC Script Templates* (10 fill-in scripts). This is a working summary in our own
words, not a copy; the PDFs stay in `~/Downloads/` for the full templates and examples.

This document is about the **script and strategy layer**. It does not change the visual
system: the look stays "Normal UGC 1" (`EDITING_SYSTEM.md`, `NORMAL_UGC_1_CONTEXT.md`)
and every hard rule in the memory file *hard-rules-no-slop* still applies. §7 says how
the two layers meet.

---

## 0. The one-page checklist — what every AI UGC ad needs

Before a script is accepted for editing, it should pass all of these.

| # | Must have | Why (source) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One desire, one message.** The ad appeals to exactly one of the six permanent desires (health, attraction, status, belonging, control, comfort) for one specific avatar. | Psychology P1: you capture desire, you never create it; two desires = nobody feels it was made for them |
| 2 | **Matched to the audience's awareness level and the ad's objective** (awareness / converting / retargeting). Never a conversion ad to a cold audience. | Strategy guide: "proposing on the first date" is the #1 mistake |
| 3 | **A hook that lands in 1–2 seconds** and does one of three jobs: creates curiosity, triggers emotion, or promises value. Native-style ads show no product and no brand in the first 3 s. | Hook Library; Raw Take (B.L.E.N.D.) |
| 4 | **Camouflage.** It looks like the content the viewer already consumes on that platform: selfie / phone-shot / casual for Reels & TikTok, screen recordings and face-to-camera for YouTube. The second it reads as an ad it is dead. | Psychology P10; Grit Ad (lo-fi outperforms studio by ~63 % in DR, per Meta) |
| 5 | **Gradualization.** Open on something the viewer already believes (a head-nod), stack two or three more agreements, then the big claim arrives as the obvious conclusion. Never open with the unbelievable promise. | Psychology P3–P4 |
| 6 | **Specificity.** Real numbers, timelines (week 1 / week 3 / month 2), named things they already tried that failed. Vague = unbelievable. | Every framework's breakdown |
| 7 | **Mechanism.** Show *how* it works so they believe *that* it works: the dashboard, the workflow, the ingredient, the demo. For software: the screen. | Psychology P8; Demo Drop (P.R.O.O.F.) |
| 8 | **Intensification.** Don't name the benefit, put them in the morning after. Paint the "new life" with sensory detail. | Psychology P5 |
| 9 | **Receipts.** Stack proof: user counts, ratings, press logos, authority quotes, before/after, testimonials. Match each claim with a proof point. | SPARK "R", STACK "C", BRIDGE "D" |
| 10 | **Objection handling by redefinition.** Don't argue the price, change what it is compared to (the chiropractor, the 7 apps, the hours lost). | Psychology P7 |
| 11 | **Concentration.** Say why every alternative they tried fails on cost / time / effort / result, so yours is the only smart option left. | Psychology P9 |
| 12 | **Identity.** Frame the product as the bridge to who they want to become ("the organised one", "the builder who ships"). | Psychology P6 |
| 13 | **A CTA that fits the tone.** Native/UGC: soft ("link's below if you want it, no pressure"). Converting: benefit-loaded with risk reversal (free tier, guarantee). Social-proof: an invitation to join, not a pitch. CTA references the hook. | all frameworks' final beat |
| 14 | **Shape and length.** ~50–60 s. Hook 0–5 s, body in 2–4 clearly separated beats of 8–15 s each, CTA in the last 8–10 s. Each beat is timecoded in the script. | every example script |

The framework chooses **how** to say it; the desire chooses **what** to say.

## 1. Strategy — where the viewer is decides which ad you make

**Five awareness levels** (Schwartz): 1 Unaware → 2 Problem-aware → 3 Solution-aware
(where most of the audience actually sits) → 4 Product-aware → 5 Most-aware.

**Three ad objectives**, what works in each, and which frameworks fit:

| Objective | Targets | What works | Measure | Frameworks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** (reach new people) | L1–L2 | entertainment/value first, pattern interrupts, education that leads to the brand, story, broad targeting | views, reach, engagement, CPM | SPARK, MYTH KILLER, RAW TAKE, EDUCATOR, PRESSURE COOKER, CLIFFHANGER |
| **Converting** (interest → action) | L2–L4 | clear demos, testimonial cascades, side-by-side, before/after, feature→benefit, strong CTA with urgency or risk reversal | CTR, CPC, CVR, ROAS | COUNTDOWN, SHORTCUT, WITNESS, DEMO DROP, TIME MACHINE, FACE-OFF, BRIDGE |
| **Retargeting** (bring them back) | L4–L5 | objection handling, social-proof stacking, limited offers, founder/BTS, "still thinking about it?", "I almost didn't buy this" | ROAS, CPA, frequency | CONVICTION, ORIGIN STORY, WITNESS, GRIT AD |

Quick match: Unaware → awareness ads · Problem-aware → awareness or converting ·
Solution-aware → converting · Product-aware → converting or retargeting · Most-aware → retargeting.

**Desire × awareness → framework** (from the psychology playbook):
health + problem-aware → PRESSURE COOKER / SHORTCUT · status + unaware → CLIFFHANGER / EDUCATOR ·
comfort + solution-aware → FACE-OFF / DEMO DROP · belonging + any → WITNESS / SPARK ·
control + problem-aware → MYTH KILLER / BRIDGE.

**Market sophistication** (how tired the market is) decides the *positioning*:
L1 new category (just say what it does) → L2 stronger claim → L3 **new mechanism** (the sweet
spot; look for products with one) → L4 better mechanism → L5 identity (nobody believes claims
any more; sell who they become). When a market hits L5 a new mechanism resets it to L3.
Find your level by listing what direct and indirect competitors claim.

## 2. Psychology — the ten principles in order of use

**Before writing:** 1 Mass desire (which of the six, for this avatar; desires are permanent,
their vehicles shift with mass education and style trends, so ride a desire on the rise) ·
2 Market sophistication · 3 Beliefs (accept them as they are; an opening line that contradicts
what the viewer knows kills the rest of the ad).

**While writing:** 4 Gradualization (known → new, one step at a time) · 5 Intensification
(make them live in it) · 6 Identification (sell who they become) · 7 Redefinition (kill
objections by changing the comparison) · 8 Mechanization (show the how) · 9 Concentration
(destroy the alternatives).

**While producing:** 10 Camouflage (match the platform's native format, energy, pacing;
simple native-looking creative is also what lets you test ten or more variants a day).

## 3. The sixteen scriptwriting frameworks

Each has: psychology, acronym, per-beat breakdown, fill-in template, a ~55 s example with
timecodes, and ten hooks. One line each here.

| Framework | Acronym → beats | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **1 SPARK** (social-proof momentum) | S.P.A.R.K. — Signal the shift · Puncture the old way · Announce the alternative · Reinforce with receipts · Kick open the door | a visible group is already switching; "join them" |
| **2 MYTH KILLER** (contrarian authority) | E.X.P.O.S.E. — Expose the lie · X-ray the problem · Present the pivot · Offer the evidence · Show the difference · Enlist the viewer | you can credibly say "what you've been told is wrong" |
| **3 COUNTDOWN** (listicle) | S.T.A.C.K. — Set the number (odd numbers win) · Tear down the familiar ("oh no, that's me") · Arrive at the answer · Certify · Kick to action | several common mistakes, one product fixes all |
| **4 ORIGIN STORY** (founder-led) | B.U.I.L.T. — Begin with the human · Uncover the enemy (a systemic problem, not a competitor) · Ignition moment · Lay out the wins (3–4 metrics) · Tomorrow's vision | trust via a person; retargeting |
| **5 SHORTCUT** (how to X without Y) | E.S.C.A.P.E. — Establish the escape · Specify the suffering · Cut to the core (one thing replaces all) · Articulate why (feature = benefit) · Paint the new life · Exit with the escape | the product removes complexity |
| **6 RAW TAKE** (native / organic) | B.L.E.N.D. — Begin like content · Lead with a relatable moment · Ease in the product ("my roommate left this…") · Narrate the experience (honest timeline incl. "week 1, nothing") · Deliver a soft close | ad blindness; the default UGC shape |
| **7 PRESSURE COOKER** (problem–agitate–relieve) | W.O.U.N.D. — Where it hurts · Open the wound (ripple effects) · Understand their failures · Name the new way (mechanism) · Deliver proof + CTA | a deep emotional pain |
| **8 GRIT AD** (lo-fi, anti-production) | 3 rules applied on top of any framework — make it SIMPLER (cut 70 % of words) · show it CHEAPLY (phone, notes-app text, crude arrows) · do the OPPOSITE of competitors | a creative filter, not a structure |
| **9 CONVICTION** (founder objection-crusher) | S.T.E.E.L. — State the harsh truth · Tell your identity · Echo the exact doubt (quote the skeptics) · Eliminate with evidence · Launch the bigger play | retargeting; warm viewers with specific doubts |
| **10 FACE-OFF** (versus / challenge) | R.I.N.G. — Raise the stakes · Initiate the showdown (split screen, timer) · Name the winner (then explain why) · Go get yours | a visible, dramatic comparison |
| **11 BRIDGE** (goal · gap · gain) | B.R.I.D.G.E. — Broadcast the goal · Reveal the real block ("it's not discipline, it's…") · Introduce the bridge · Detail the proof · Glimpse the transformation · Encourage action | the viewer already wants the outcome |
| **12 CLIFFHANGER** (curiosity loop) | L.O.O.P. — Launch the impossible claim · Orchestrate the story · Offer the value inside it · Pay off the promise | retention; a claim that demands explanation |
| **13 WITNESS** (testimonial cascade) | V.O.I.C.E. — Verify the pain (customer voice) · Open the discovery · Illustrate the change (timeline) · Cascade 4–6 quick-cut voices · Endorse & encourage (the first voice returns for the CTA) | third-party proof; many "people" (AI-generated or real) |
| **14 DEMO DROP** (product in action) | P.R.O.O.F. — Problem in progress (show, don't tell) · Reveal · Operate in real time (one continuous shot, minimal VO) · Observe the result (hold) · Frame the features (why it worked) | seeing is believing; software = screen |
| **15 TIME MACHINE** (before / after) | S.H.I.F.T. — Show the starting point · Highlight the breaking point · Introduce the change (one tool, one decision) · Flash forward (same specificity as the before) · Transfer the possibility | contrast as proof |
| **16 EDUCATOR** (value-first teaching) | T.E.A.C.H. — Teach something real · Expand with depth · Align with the product · Credentialize · Hand off the action | highest-trust format; gives before it asks |

**Pairings that work:** SPARK + COUNTDOWN · MYTH KILLER + PRESSURE COOKER · ORIGIN STORY +
CONVICTION · RAW TAKE + WITNESS · SHORTCUT + BRIDGE · CLIFFHANGER + DEMO DROP · EDUCATOR +
SPARK · GRIT on top of anything.

**Static companions** (for feed tests): Offer card · Golden review · Editorial callout ("as seen
in") · Notes-app screenshot (the static Grit ad) · Side-by-side.

## 4. UGC script shapes — the shared skeleton

The ten fill-in templates (Unboxing, Testimonial, Day-in-my-life, Problem–Solution,
Before/After, GRWM, Comparison/Review, Street interview, Tutorial/How-to, Story time) all
reduce to one skeleton, which is also what makes a script sound like a person and not a brand:

1. **Hook** that sounds like talking to a friend ("I never do these but…", "if you deal with X, stop scrolling").
2. **Before state**, specific and a little embarrassing.
3. **Things already tried** that failed.
4. **Skeptical discovery** ("someone told me about… I was like, really?").
5. **Honest timeline** with the first result, then the big one, in concrete metrics.
6. **What makes it different** (the mechanism / USP), often against a named alternative.
7. **Soft CTA** with a low-risk line ("worst case you…, best case…").

Tutorial/How-to for software: "this is stupid easy" → 3 steps, each shown → result → the
common mistake and the fix → "I use this for X and it saves me Y every Z".
Street-interview works for AI UGC too: generate different people reacting, save the best for last.

## 5. Hooks

Three jobs: **curiosity**, **emotion**, or **value promise**. Six families in the library:

- **Curiosity** — the part they don't show you; I tested this for 30 days; you've been doing X wrong; what nobody tells you about X.
- **Shock / controversy** — unpopular opinion; stop doing X, here's why; if you're still doing X in 2026 we need to talk; everyone is lying to you about X.
- **Value / educational** — how to X in [short time]; 3 things I wish I knew before X; do this instead of X; why your X isn't working and how to fix it.
- **Story / emotional** — a year ago I was X, today Y; I almost gave up, then this; I have a confession.
- **AI-video specific** — AI made this, can you tell?; this would have cost $50k two years ago; you don't need a camera any more, here's proof; the AI tool nobody is talking about yet.
- **Engagement / CTA** — comment [word] for the guide; follow for part 2; save this; rate it 1–10.

Rule from the library: adapt the pattern, never the exact words; the bracketed structure is
what matters. Every framework in §3 also ships ten hooks in its own voice.

## 6. Testing posture

AVB's operating assumption: you don't know which belief, pain or redefinition converts, so
you make many cheap native-looking variants (ten-plus video ads a day with AI, hundreds a
week at AVB) and let the feedback loop decide. The hook and the first beat are where variants
differ; the body and CTA can stay. Measure per objective (§1).

## 7. How this meets "Normal UGC 1" (fanout.sh reels)

The AVB material is about **what the presenter says and in what order**. Our system is about
**how the finished reel looks**. They join at the plan: the script's beats become the title
stack, the keyword, the screens and the logo pops. Nothing here licenses new graphic devices
(see *hard-rules-no-slop*); the camouflage principle actually argues for our restrained,
human, typographic look over "ad graphics".

**Script intake (new step before editing).** For each new A-roll + script, note:
desire (one of six) · awareness level of the audience · objective · framework(s) used ·
market-sophistication level (fanout.sh is most likely selling a *new mechanism*, L3, to a
solution-aware audience, which favours SHORTCUT, DEMO DROP, BRIDGE and FACE-OFF; confirm per script).
Then run the §0 checklist and flag gaps to Abik *before* the preview: a missing mechanism beat,
no receipts, a hook that names the product in the first 3 s, a CTA that doesn't reference the hook.

**Mapping beats to the existing grammar (no new elements):**

| Script beat | Normal UGC 1 element |
|---|---|
| The one desire / the claim word | the SF Pro keyword in the rose box; one per title stack |
| Framework beats (the number in a COUNTDOWN, the "without X" of a SHORTCUT, the before/after of a TIME MACHINE) | Instrument Serif Italic title lines, under the chin or above the head |
| Mechanism / demo (P.R.O.O.F., T.E.A.C.H. "align") | real 9:16 browser recordings with the cursor, matching the spoken words, cutout overlapping the bottom |
| Receipts / social proof (SPARK "R", STACK "C") | logo pops; stacked screenshot slides or list scrolls of real reviews / users |
| Relatable moment / intensification | movie b-roll full screen with the cutout and the title stack above the head |
| Hook (first 3 s, no product) | full-frame presenter, title under the chin; no screen yet |
| Soft CTA | presenter full frame; outro loader plays out to the last frame |

**Variants.** When Abik wants ad tests, the cheap variant is a different *hook take* (first 5 s
of A-roll) on the same body and the same plan, not a different graphic treatment.

## 8. The "Script" trigger — reference in, fanout script out

Abik says **"Script"** and pastes a reference (a reel, a brand film, a TikTok). That is the whole brief.
The job: a script that stops the scroll the way the reference does, in fanout's voice, with every rule above handled.

**1. Decode the reference before writing a word.** Hook type and why it stops the scroll; beat
structure with timings; voice (person, tense, temperature); typography and visual grammar; music
energy; how the tagline lands. Get the voiceover verbatim (captions / auto-subs), not from memory.

**2. Intake (§1–§3).** One desire (fanout: status, "what senior AI engineers know that I don't"),
awareness level, objective, framework(s), sophistication. fanout is L5: it is "not a course", so the
word *course* never describes fanout.

**3. Similar, not a copy.** Keep what makes the reference work: the structure, the energy, the rhythm,
the puncture moment. Change the words, the images and the argument to fanout's own. Defaults:
first-person, warm, a presenter telling a friend; identity word **"Engineers"**, not "Senior";
short and on the point (15–40 s, about three words a second, one job per beat); no product in the
first 3 s; CTA "start at the first branch, it's free. fanout.sh".

**4. Verify every claim on fanout.sh** before it goes in (20K+ engineers, weekly maintenance, free
tracks, the 18k req/s Labs instrument, the logo strip).

**5. Deliver as a `.md` next to `Fanout anthem script.md`:** keep/change table vs the reference,
intake block, timecoded script table with a shot column in Normal UGC 1 grammar (§7: title lines,
rose-box keyword in bold, pill captions, recordings, b-roll with cutout, logo pops, white card +
loader), a 15-second cut, two or three swap-in hooks, a rules-check table, a claims list.
Existing elements only; new graphics need Abik's sign-off.

Worked example: `Fanout anthem script.md` ("Mapped.", from the Base44 "Can't is dead" film, 2026-10-29).
